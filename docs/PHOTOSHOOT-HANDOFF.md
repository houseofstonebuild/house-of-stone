# House of Stone — Monday photography handoff

The website templates are prepared for the final selection, whether it is fifteen works or another number. Existing IDs and collection records are retained; final works can replace photographs and content, or unused records can be archived. Do not rename source-page references until each physical sculpture is matched.

## Set and art direction

Use a real soft, warm off-white seamless backdrop and a stable matte pedestal that is suitable for the sculpture's weight. Keep a quiet contact shadow, neutral white balance, room around the whole silhouette and a consistent camera height. Avoid strong reflections, clipped pale edges and orange color casts. Photograph the complete sculpture and any included base. Do not crop fingers, tips, wings or supports.

Capture a color-chart reference under the same lighting and a ruler/scale reference separately. Keep reference photographs out of the public gallery. Retain the original camera files and deliver full-resolution JPEGs alongside RAW originals, without messaging-app compression.

## Per sculpture

Use an identifying card and a temporary shoot ID, such as HOS-001. Record the original ZSA page when applicable. Make one folder per sculpture.

- `front.jpg`, `left.jpg`, `right.jpg`, `back.jpg`: complete, sharply focused views at matching scale and lighting.
- A three-quarter beauty view is useful in addition to the four factual views.
- `detail.jpg`: grain, polish and texture.
- `signature.jpg`: if a signature is present.
- `scale.jpg`: an optional view showing the physical size.
- Photograph removable bases and any relevant condition details separately.

The website has named slots for these photographs, a thumbnail gallery and a full-resolution inspection viewer. Missing views remain unpublished until supplied.

## 3D capture is a separate sequence

Four cardinal photographs are not enough for reliable photogrammetry. Keep the sculpture stationary and move the camera around it when practical. Shoot overlapping rings at low, middle and high elevations, with extra views into openings and around overhangs. As a practical planning target, allow roughly 72–120 sharp images per work, adjusting for complexity; maintain at least approximately 70% overlap. These counts are a capture plan, not a guarantee of reconstruction quality.

Use consistent diffuse lighting, focus, exposure, white balance and focal length. Do not apply portrait blur, background replacement or other image generation to the reconstruction sequence. Preserve the native images and their metadata. Include measured dimensions or a known scale reference for sizing the reconstructed model.

Polished, dark or featureless stone can be difficult to reconstruct because reflections move between views. Ask the photographer or scanning operator about polarized lighting and a test reconstruction. If using a turntable, the reconstruction workflow needs to account for the stationary background through masking. Do not apply coatings or sprays to the sculptures for capture.

Test one representative polished sculpture before spending the entire session on the same method. Prioritize complete capture of the five homepage stars if time is limited. Final 3D approval depends on the complete shape, openings, depth, sides, reverse and texture—not a single matching front view.

Reference guidance:

- [Epic: Photogrammetry camera movement](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/realityscan-mobile/Photogrammetry-Camera-Movement?lang=en-US)
- [Agisoft: Close-range objects scanning](https://agisoft.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/31000149340-close-range-objects-scanning)
- [Agisoft: Turntable workflow](https://agisoft.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/31000163268-turntable-capturing-scenario-and-processing-workflow)

## Per artist

- A solo portrait, including a portrait crop with room around the head and shoulders.
- A wide photograph beside an identifiable sculpture; include the full work and enough space for responsive framing.
- Full preferred name and spelling, brief biography, artistic practice, a short quotation in their own words, and the works they made.
- One or two specific details about their approach, experience and relationship to stone are more useful than generic promotional language.

The profile template includes portrait, biography, practice, artist quotation, studio image and works linked across collections.

## Process photographs and films

Capture about ten photographs across stone selection, the initial block, rough shaping, carving, openings, refining, sanding, polishing, signature and the finished work. Keep the real tools and hands visible against an uncluttered background. Capture wide scenes and close details with consistent color.

The Making supports an ordered sequence of ten photographs and captions. Deliver the approximately five motion clips separately, with a short description and the artist/work IDs. Motion placement is intentionally deferred; the files need not be uploaded to the site yet.

## Sculpture information

For each shoot ID, provide:

- Official title, artist and intended primary collection.
- Stone type and sourcing information that the artist can confirm.
- Year, description or story, finish and signature information.
- Height × width × depth in centimeters, weight in kilograms, and base dimensions. State whether each dimension includes the base and whether the base is included.
- Current status: unknown, available, reserved or sold; current location.
- Condition and documented authenticity/provenance information.
- Work-specific care, delivery, shipping and installation details where confirmed.

The website calculates inch and pound equivalents. Leave unknown fields empty. Prices remain on inquiry. Original ZSA page 13 retains its confirmed Sold status.

## Replacement workflow

1. Match shoot IDs to the records and confirm the final selection.
2. Replace the main image, angle images, artist portrait/studio image and process sequence in the existing draft.
3. Replace the working descriptions and specifications, confirming sourced fields individually.
4. Build or ingest the matching 3D assets from the capture sequences, and review all angles before enabling them publicly.
5. Preview desktop and mobile, check sculpture-specific inquiries, and publish through the existing editor.

The production database and uploaded masters remain on the existing persistent volume. Content replacement does not require a database reset or a new website.
