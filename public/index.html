THE MAKING

From stone
to sculpture.

A dialogue between material, instinct and the hand. Each surface shaped with intention.

01 / MATERIAL

The stone.

Colour, grain and natural character give each work its starting point. The material invites a form rather than simply receiving one.

02 / PRACTICE

The hand.

Carving moves from broad decisions to patient refinements. A curve, a hollow and a change in texture reveal the artist's individual approach.

03 / EXPRESSION

The form.

Polished and natural surfaces meet in the finished work. Light brings their differences into focus, and the stone takes on a presence of its own.

FORM MEETS ARCHITECTURE

A room, transformed
by one considered work.

Stone introduces a different rhythm into an interior. Its weight grounds a room; its surface catches the changing light. A single work can bring focus to a living space, an entrance or a quiet corner.

We invite interior designers and architects to explore the collection with the setting in mind. Share your brief, dimensions and reference images, and we can begin a thoughtful conversation about the works that speak to your project.

01 / SCALE

The right proportion.

Consider the height of a console, the depth of a niche and the space around a plinth. Dimensions begin the conversation.

02 / MATERIAL

A material dialogue.

Pale stone, green serpentine and dark springstone each bring a distinct tone to wood, plaster, metal and natural light.

03 / PLACEMENT

Room to breathe.

A sculpture needs space to be seen from more than one angle. Consider sightlines, lighting and the experience of moving through the room.

LIVING WITH SCULPTURE

Collected slowly.
Experienced every day.

A collection can begin with one work: a form you return to, a material that holds your attention, an artist whose practice resonates. In a home gallery or a living room, sculpture rewards a closer look.

Explore the artists, take time with the works and ask questions. We want the conversation to begin with what draws you to a piece, whether you are discovering stone sculpture for the first time or considering a new direction for an established collection.

01 / DISCOVER

OUR VISION

A considered home
for Zimbabwean sculpture.

We believe Zimbabwean stone sculpture deserves to be encountered with the attention given to any great work of art: through its form, its material and the individual practice of its maker.

House of Stone brings those elements into focus. Our vision is to connect artists in Zimbabwe with collectors and designers who value expressive form, patient craftsmanship and work that carries a sense of place into a contemporary setting.

BRADLEY & MILTON

Two founders.
A shared perspective.

House of Stone is the work of Bradley and Milton, brought together by a shared interest in Zimbabwean artistry and how it can be presented to a wider audience.

From Zimbabwe to New York, we are building an art house around a simple idea: let the work speak, make space for the artist, and bring care to the experience of discovery. The collection is the beginning of that conversation.

Privacy.

Your enquiry.

When you send an enquiry, House of Stone receives your name, email address, interest, and message so we can respond. Enquiries are kept in a private inbox. If email notifications are enabled, they are also delivered through our email provider, Resend. Your details are used for this conversation and are not added to a marketing list.

Understanding interest.

We measure page visits, collection clicks, work views, and enquiries using a random identifier for your browser session. Activity records contain artwork identifiers and campaign information, rather than the contents of your enquiry. We do not store your IP address in activity records. Activity records are removed after one year.

Other services.

The Manrope typeface is loaded from Google Fonts. Your browser preference is stored on your device. Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control signals turn activity measurement off.

Questions or removal.

Send an enquiry if you want to ask about your information or request that an earlier enquiry be removed.
